You might say it

every week without knowing

what it means.

Amen.

It's often translated:

“so be it.” But

in Hebrew,

amen comes from a

root that means solid,

firm,

reliable.

The same root also

gives us the words

faith and truth.

In Exodus,

during a battle,

Moses keeps his arms

raised.

Aaron and Hur hold

up his hands,

and the text says

they were steady until

sunset.

The prophet Isaiah even

plays on it:

be established.” In Hebrew,

believing and standing firm

are almost the same

word.

When Ezra reads the

Law,

all the people answer,

lifting their hands:

“Amen!

Amen!” So saying amen

isn't just making a

wish.

It's saying:

this is solid,

I'm leaning on it.

Here's the surprising part:

Jesus uses it backwards.

Instead of saying it

at the end,

he puts it at

the beginning:

“Amen,

I tell you.” Our

Bibles translate:

I tell you.” And

in Revelation,

Jesus himself is called

the Amen,

the faithful and true

witness.

The word passed unchanged

into Greek,

Latin,

Arabic,

and many other languages.

Next time you say

Amen,

remember:

you're saying this is

solid.

Which Bible word should

I explain next?

Tell me in the

comments.
