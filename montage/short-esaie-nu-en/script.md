This prophet walked naked and barefoot for three years. On God's orders.
It's not a legend: it's Isaiah, chapter twenty.
Around 711 BC, the Assyrian army captures Ashdod, a neighbouring city. In Jerusalem, some want to look to Egypt for help.
Here is the scene, in the World English Bible: "Go, and loosen the sackcloth from off your waist, and take your shoes from off your feet." He did so, walking naked and barefoot.
What does "naked" mean? The Hebrew word can describe someone without a cloak, in a simple tunic. Scholars debate it.
And the three years? Depending on the translation, either Isaiah walks for three years, or the sign lasts three years. The text is ambiguous.
The meaning, however, is clear. God explains: the king of Assyria will lead away captives from Egypt and Ethiopia, naked and barefoot.
The message: do not put your hope in Egypt.
Among the prophets, a gesture sometimes speaks louder than a speech. Jeremiah wears a yoke, Ezekiel draws a siege.
