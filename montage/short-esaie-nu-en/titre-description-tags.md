## ➤ Video description : 

A prophet who walks "naked and barefoot" on God's orders: not a legend, but Isaiah 20. In one minute, discover the context (Assyria takes Ashdod around 711 BC), what the text says, what it leaves open, and the message to Judah.

The Hebrew word translated "naked" can describe someone without a cloak, in a simple tunic: scholars debate it. The "three years" are ambiguous too: depending on the translation, either Isaiah walks for three years, or the sign lasts three years. The meaning is clear: the king of Assyria will lead away captives from Egypt and Ethiopia. The message: do not put your hope in Egypt. Among the prophets, a gesture sometimes speaks louder than a speech (Jeremiah and his yoke, Ezekiel and his siege).

📖 KEY VERSE: "Go, and loosen the sackcloth from off your waist, and take your shoes from off your feet." He did so, walking naked and barefoot. — Isaiah 20:2 (WEB)
