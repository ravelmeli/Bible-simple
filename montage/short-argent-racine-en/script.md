"Money is the root of all evil." That sentence is everywhere, especially on Black Friday. But the Bible does not say it that way.
It comes from 1 Timothy 6, verse 10. In the World English Bible: "For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil."
First detail: it is not "money," it is "the love of money."
Second detail: it is not "the" root, but "a" root, among others.
The verse goes on: "Some have been led astray from the faith in their greed, and have pierced themselves through with many sorrows."
Jesus says the same thing in Luke 12, verse 15: "Beware! Keep yourselves from covetousness, for a man's life doesn't consist of the abundance of the things which he possesses."
And Hebrews 13, verse 5: "Be free from the love of money, content with such things as you have."
These texts do not condemn money in itself. They warn against attachment to it.
So the real question is not "am I allowed to buy?" but "what owns me?"
The text invites contentment, not guilt.
