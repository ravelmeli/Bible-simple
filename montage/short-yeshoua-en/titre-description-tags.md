## ➤ Video description : 

Jesus wasn't called "Jesus": his family and friends said Yeshua. Find out in one minute where the name we use comes from, how Yeshua became Iēsous in Greek, Iesus in Latin, then Jesus, Jésus or Gesù.

Yeshua is a short form of Yehoshua, which we translate "Joshua," and this name is generally understood as "the Lord saves." Matthew 1:21 gives the reason. The Gospels are written in Greek, where the final "s" is a Greek ending; in Greek, Joshua is also called Iēsous: it is the same name. In the first century, Yeshua was very common in Judea, hence "of Nazareth." Saying "Jesus" is therefore not a mistake: it is the same name, passed from one language to another. That Jesus spoke Aramaic, and probably Hebrew too, is the common scholarly view.
