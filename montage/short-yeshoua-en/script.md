Jesus wasn't called "Jesus." His family and friends said Yeshua.
He speaks Aramaic, the everyday language in Galilee, and probably Hebrew too.
His name is Yeshua, a short form of Yehoshua, which we translate "Joshua."
This name generally means "the Lord saves."
Matthew gives the reason, in the World English Bible: "She will give birth to a son. You shall call his name Jesus, for it is he who will save his people from their sins."
But the Gospels are written in Greek. There, Yeshua becomes Iēsous, with a final "s": a Greek ending.
Latin makes it Iesus, then come Jesus, Jésus, Gesù.
A surprising detail: in Greek, Joshua is also called Iēsous. It is the same name.
In the first century, Yeshua is very common in Judea. So people added: "of Nazareth."
So, are we wrong to say "Jesus"? Of course not: it is the same name, passed from one language to another.
But knowing he was called Yeshua, "the Lord saves," gives that name its full meaning.
