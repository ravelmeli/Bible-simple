Which apostle had the nickname "the twin"? Thomas.
In the Gospel of John, we read: "Thomas, who is called Didymus."
Two names for one idea. "Thomas" comes from Aramaic, "Didymus" from Greek, and both mean "twin."
Twin of whom? The Bible does not say.
Ancient non-biblical writings, like the Acts of Thomas, make him the twin of Jesus. It's a late tradition, with no support in the Gospels.
He is often called "the doubter," because of John 20. He refuses to believe in the resurrection without seeing and touching the wounds of Jesus.
Eight days later, Jesus says to him, in the World English Bible: "Because you have seen me, you have believed. Blessed are those who have not seen, and have believed."
But Thomas is also a courageous disciple. In John 11, he says to the others: "Let's go also, that we may die with him."
And he makes the strongest confession of the Gospel: "My Lord and my God!"
