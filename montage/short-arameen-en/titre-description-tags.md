## ➤ Description de la video : 

The Gospels are written in Greek. Yet Jesus spoke Aramaic. In one minute, discover the words the Gospel writers kept just as he said them.

In first-century Palestine, Aramaic was the language of everyday life. The Gospel writers wrote in Greek, but they preserved a few words in Jesus' own language: "Talitha cumi," which Mark translates "Girl, I tell you, get up!" (Mark 5:41); "Ephphatha," "Be opened" (Mark 7:34); "Abba," "Father," in prayer at Gethsemane (Mark 14:36); and on the cross, "Eloi, Eloi, lama sabachthani," "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" (Mark 15:34). Did Jesus also know Hebrew and a little Greek? Many historians think so, but it's a hypothesis: the text doesn't say. The surprising part: his most intimate words, at a child's bedside, in prayer, and on the cross, stayed in his mother tongue.
