## ➤ Description de la video : 

"I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me." You see it on tattoos, on jerseys, before a game or an exam. But Paul wasn't talking about winning. In a minute and a half, discover what Philippians 4:13 really says.

When Paul wrote these words, he was in prison. He had already known beatings, shipwrecks, hunger and cold (2 Corinthians 11), and he was writing to the Christians in Philippi, who had just sent him help through Epaphroditus. Right before this verse, he writes: "I have learned to be content in whatever circumstances I am. I know how to be humbled, and I also know how to abound. In any and all circumstances I have learned the secret of being filled and of going hungry." Paul's "all things" means standing firm in every situation, even the worst. The surprising part: the Greek verb translated "I have learned" (memyēmai) literally means "I have been initiated," as if contentment were a secret you discover by walking through hunger. And in the very next verse, Paul thanks his friends for sharing in his affliction: his strength doesn't come from himself alone. This verse doesn't promise victory, but the strength to hold on, whether you win or lose.
