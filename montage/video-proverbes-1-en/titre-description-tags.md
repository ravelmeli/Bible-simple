## ➤ Video Description: 

Where do you begin, to understand the whole Bible? There are reading plans, summaries and commentaries, and still you sometimes feel that one key is missing. In this study, we see why Proverbs 1 can play that role: not as the only key, but as a key to reading that the Bible itself gives us.

It all starts with a scene: a woman is shouting in the middle of the marketplace, with no stall, no army and no title. This woman is Wisdom. We first place the book in its context: Solomon reigns around the tenth century BC, the text itself says that men of King Hezekiah copied out more of Solomon's proverbs, and several scholars point to similarities with an Egyptian wisdom text, the Instruction of Amenemope.

We then read the opening verses, and three Hebrew words that shed light on the whole book: chokhmah (wisdom, also the skill of a craftsman), musar (instruction, discipline) and yirah (fear). In verse 7, "The fear of Yahweh is the beginning of knowledge" sets an order: we don't begin to understand by placing ourselves at the center, but by recognizing God in his place.

The chapter then unfolds in two scenes: a father warns his son about men who want to lure him, and then Wisdom herself speaks in the streets and promises security to whoever listens. Finally, we see how this idea runs through Psalm 111, Job 28 and Ecclesiastes, and then the New Testament, with Matthew 12, 1 Corinthians 1 and James 1. We honestly separate what the text says from what Christian tradition adds: reading Jesus as the wisdom of God is a Christian reading, one that Jewish readers of Proverbs do not make.

📖 KEY VERSE: "The fear of Yahweh is the beginning of knowledge; but the foolish despise wisdom and instruction." — Proverbs 1:7, World English Bible
