## ➤ Video description: 

"Christ" is not Jesus' last name. In his time, in Judea, people did not use family names the way we do today: they said "Jesus of Nazareth" or "the son of Joseph." "Jesus" is the Greek form of Yehoshua, a common Hebrew name meaning "the Lord saves."

"Christ" is a title: it comes from the Greek "Christos," "anointed," which translates the Hebrew "Mashiach," the Messiah. When Peter says "You are the Christ, the Son of the living God" (Matthew 16:16), he is recognizing a title. "Jesus Christ" means "Jesus, the Messiah": a statement of faith. Over time the word came to be used almost like a name, especially in Paul's letters, and the disciples were first called "Christians" in Antioch (Acts 11:26).
