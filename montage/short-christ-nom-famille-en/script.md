"Christ" is not Jesus' last name. "Jesus Christ" is not written like "John Smith."
In his time, in Judea, people did not use family names the way we do today. They said "Jesus of Nazareth," or "the son of Joseph."
"Jesus" really is his name. In Matthew 1, verse 21, in the World English Bible, the angel tells Joseph: "She shall give birth to a son. You shall call his name Jesus, for it is he who shall save his people from their sins."
Jesus is the Greek form of Yehoshua, a very common Hebrew name, which means "the Lord saves."
"Christ," on the other hand, is not a name but a title. It comes from the Greek "Christos," which means "anointed."
It translates the Hebrew "Mashiach," the Messiah: the one who was anointed with oil, like kings and priests.
When Peter says, in Matthew 16, verse 16: "You are the Christ, the Son of the living God," he is not giving a last name. He is recognizing a title.
"Jesus Christ" therefore means "Jesus, the Messiah." It is already a statement of faith.
Over time, the word came to be used almost like a name, especially in the letters of Paul.
And in Acts 11, verse 26: "The disciples were first called Christians in Antioch." The people of the Christ.
