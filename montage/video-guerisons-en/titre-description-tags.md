## ➤ Video description: 

One evening at Capernaum, a whole city gathers at a single door to bring its sick to Jesus. But how many healings do the Gospels really tell, one by one? In this study, we go through the healing accounts sorted by type of illness: the skin, sight, hearing and speech, movement, fever and blood, unclean spirits, and death.

For each category, we look at what the text says and what Jesus does exactly: the lepers of Mark 1 and Luke 17 (and what the word "leprosy" really covers in the Bible), Bartimaeus at Jericho, the blind man of Bethsaida healed in two stages, the man born blind in John 9, the deaf man and the Aramaic word "Ephphatha," the paralytic lowered through the roof, the man at Bethesda, the bent woman, Peter's mother-in-law, the royal official's son, the centurion's servant, the woman who bled for twelve years, then the accounts of unclean spirits, and finally Jairus's daughter, the widow's son at Nain, and Lazarus.

We then compare the gestures (a word, a touch, mud, saliva, distance), the place of faith, and the passage in Mark 6:5 where Jesus does not heal everyone. Jesus' answer to John the Baptist (Matthew 11:5) takes up almost exactly these categories, echoing the promises of Isaiah. We also say honestly what the text does not say: it makes no medical diagnosis, it does not promise that every believer will be healed, and the New Testament also speaks of sick people who are not healed (Paul's thorn, Trophimus at Miletus).

One sentence finally reminds us that these accounts do not replace treatment: seeing a doctor does not oppose faith.

📖 KEY VERSE: "the blind receive their sight, and the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, and the deaf hear, the dead are raised up, and the poor have good news preached to them." — Matthew 11:5, World English Bible
