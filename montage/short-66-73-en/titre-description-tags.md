## ➤ Description de la video : 

A Catholic Bible has seven more books than a Protestant Bible: sixty-six books on one side, seventy-three on the other. In one minute, find out why.

The New Testament is the same everywhere: twenty-seven books. The difference is in the Old Testament. Protestants follow the Hebrew Bible, thirty-nine books. Catholics also follow the Septuagint, an ancient Greek translation, which has seven more: Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, and the two books of Maccabees. In the sixteenth century, Luther set them apart, as useful reading but outside the Bible. In 1546, the Council of Trent declared them canonical. The Orthodox Churches count even more, depending on their traditions. The surprising part: Jerome, the translator of the Vulgate, knew they weren't in the Hebrew Bible, but he included them anyway. Catholics call these books "deuterocanonical," Protestants call them "apocrypha": this video presents both positions without taking sides.
