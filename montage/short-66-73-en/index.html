04 Lesson

A Catholic Bible has

seven more books than

a Protestant Bible,

sixty-six books on one

side,

seventy-three on the other.

Why?

The New Testament is

the same everywhere,

twenty-seven books.

The difference is in

the Old Testament.

Protestants follow the Hebrew

Bible,

thirty-nine books.

Catholics also follow the

Septuagint,

an ancient Greek translation

which has seven more.

Tobit,

Judith,

Wisdom,

Sirach,

Baruch,

and the two books

of Maccabees.

In the sixteenth century,

Luther sets them apart

as useful reading,

but outside the Bible.

In 1546,

the Council of Trent

declares them canonical.

The Orthodox Churches count

even more,

depending on their traditions.

Here's the surprising part.

Jerome,

the translator of the

Vulgate,

knew they weren't in

the Hebrew Bible,

but he included them

anyway.
