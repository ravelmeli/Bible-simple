Nothing in the Bible says that Mary Magdalene was a prostitute.
Her name comes from Magdala, a village on the shore of the Sea of Galilee.
The first text that mentions her is Luke 8, in the World English Bible: "Mary who was called Magdalene, from whom seven demons had gone out."
The text speaks of seven demons, not of sins, and never of prostitution.
So where does the idea come from? From a mix-up. In Luke 7, a sinful woman, whose name is not given, anoints the feet of Jesus.
In 591, Pope Gregory the Great identified the two women in a homily. That reading shaped the West for centuries.
But no biblical passage identifies them. The Catholic Church itself stopped confusing them in its liturgical calendar, in 1969.
What the Gospels say: she follows Jesus, she is present at the cross, and she is the first to see the risen Christ, in John 20.
In 2016, Pope Francis honored her with the title "apostle of the apostles."
