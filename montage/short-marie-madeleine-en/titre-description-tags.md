## ➤ Video description : 

Nothing in the Bible says that Mary Magdalene was a prostitute. Her name comes from Magdala, a village in Galilee. The first text that mentions her is Luke 8:2: "Mary who was called Magdalene, from whom seven demons had gone out". The text speaks of seven demons, not of sins, and never of prostitution.

The idea comes from a mix-up with the unnamed sinful woman of Luke 7, who anoints the feet of Jesus. In 591, Pope Gregory the Great identified the two women in a homily, and that reading shaped the West for centuries. No biblical passage identifies them, and the Catholic Church stopped confusing them in its liturgical calendar in 1969. The Gospels show Mary of Magdala following Jesus, present at the cross, and the first to see the risen Christ (John 20). In 2016, Pope Francis honored her with the title "apostle of the apostles".
