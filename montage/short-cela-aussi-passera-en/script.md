"This too shall pass." Almost everyone thinks that sentence is in the Bible. It isn't.
It does not appear in any book of the Bible, neither in the Old Testament nor in the New.
So where does it come from? From a legend about King Solomon, found in Jewish and Persian traditions.
In these stories, a king looks for a sentence that stays true in joy as well as in sorrow. He is given that one.
In the nineteenth century, the phrase spreads in English, notably in a speech by Abraham Lincoln in 1859.
What the Bible says is different. In Ecclesiastes 3, in the World English Bible: "For everything there is a season, and a time for every purpose under heaven."
And in Psalm 30: "Weeping may stay for the night, but joy comes in the morning."
The text does not promise that everything passes quickly. It says that pain has its time, and that it does not have the last word.
Only one thing does not pass, according to Isaiah 40: "The word of our God stands forever."
If your pain lasts, talk to a loved one or to a professional: it is not a lack of faith.
