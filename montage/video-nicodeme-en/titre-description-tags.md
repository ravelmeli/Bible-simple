## ➤ Video description :

Nicodemus is a Pharisee, a notable, a respected man. One evening he goes to see Jesus, but at night. What does the Gospel of John really say about him? In this study we follow his three scenes: the night visit (John 3), his defense before the council (John 7) and the burial of Jesus (John 19), step by step, distinguishing what the text says, the hypotheses and the tradition.

We see why "by night" remains a riddle (fear, discretion or symbol; in John, night and light matter), the double meaning of the Greek word anōthen ("again" or "from above"), the wind and the Spirit, the bronze serpent, Nicodemus' sentence before his colleagues, then his gesture at the foot of the cross, with about a hundred pounds of myrrh and aloes. The text never says that Nicodemus became a disciple: it is tradition that kept him as a believer, and the "Gospel of Nicodemus" is a much later apocryphal writing.
