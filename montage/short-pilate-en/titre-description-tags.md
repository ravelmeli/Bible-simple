## ➤ Video description : 

In 1961, Italian archaeologists turn over a block of limestone at Caesarea, reused as a stair step in the Roman theater, and read a Latin inscription: "Pontius Pilatus, prefect of Judea." Find out in under two minutes what this stone says, what it shows, and what it doesn't show.

The first line of the inscription is incomplete: many scholars read "Tiberieum," a building dedicated to the emperor Tiberius, but that reading is still debated. The stone gives Pilate's official title, prefect, while Luke writes "governor," a more general word. Josephus, Philo and Tacitus also mention him. The stone shows that a Roman prefect named Pontius Pilate existed, in the place and time the Gospels give; it shows nothing about the trial of Jesus, which is a matter of texts.

📖 KEY VERSE: "Now in the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar, Pontius Pilate being governor of Judea." — Luke 3:1 (WEB)
