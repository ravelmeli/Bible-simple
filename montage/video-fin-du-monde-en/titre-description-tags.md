## ➤ Video description: 

The end of the world has already been announced, and postponed, many times. Today, it is the prophecies for 2027 and the quatrains of Nostradamus that are going around, and many believers wonder, sometimes with anxiety: does the Bible say when it will all end? In this study, we look at what the text says, what history says, and what Jesus answered to exactly this question.

We begin with Nostradamus: who Michel de Nostredame was, what The Prophecies (1555) are, why historians say the verses are so vague that they are recognized only after the fact, and where a fake quatrain wrongly attributed to Nostradamus came from. Then we see how the Bible judges a prophecy, with the test of Deuteronomy 18 and the example of Jonah at Nineveh.

We then read Jesus' discourse in Matthew 24: the disciples' question, the Greek phrase synteleia tou aionos ("the completion of an age"), the destruction of the temple in the year 70, and the central saying of Matthew 24:36: "But no one knows of that day and hour…" We set it beside Acts 1:7, Matthew 24:4-6, 1 Thessalonians 5 and 2 Peter 3.

We also walk through the history of announced dates: William Miller and the Great Disappointment of 1844, the legend of the panic of the year one thousand, the Maya calendar of 2012. Finally, we read the Apocalypse (apokalypsis: unveiling, revelation), see what parousia means, and offer three simple questions to ask when you meet an end-of-the-world prophecy. One sentence also reminds us that a doctor, a therapist or a friend can help when anxiety takes up too much room.

📖 KEY VERSE: "But no one knows of that day and hour, not even the angels of heaven, nor the Son, but the Father alone." — Matthew 24:36, World English Bible
